What to Text Someone Whose Pet Died

✨
The Vibe Check Project Advice & Guides

A pet is there for the ordinary parts of someone's life: every morning, every walk, every night on the couch. When that pet dies, the loss shows up everywhere at once. The empty food bowl. The leash by the door. The quiet when they walk in.

A lot of people grieving a pet also feel like they aren't allowed to be this sad. They worry others will think they're overreacting, so they keep it to themselves. That's exactly why a text from you matters.

Your message only needs to do two things: treat the loss as real, and make space for their pet, not rush past it.

Related Reading: If you found this helpful, you might also like our guide on How to Comfort Someone Who Lost a Loved One.

7 Texts to Send Someone Whose Pet Died

1. Say Their Pet's Name

Using the pet's name tells them you see this as the loss of someone, not something. Swap in the name before you send it.

"I'm so sorry about [Name]. They were family, and I know how much you loved them. I'm thinking of you."

2. Give Their Grief Permission

Many pet owners quietly feel embarrassed by how hard this is hitting them. Tell them it makes sense.

Don't just read it. Send it.

"Losing a pet is losing a family member. However hard this feels is exactly how hard it is. You never have to explain that to me."
Send as Card ✨

3. Invite a Memory

Most people want to talk about their pet, but don't want to bring it up. Give them an easy opening, with no pressure to take it right away.

"What's your favorite thing [Name] used to do? I'd love to hear about them, whenever you feel like it."
Send as Card ✨

4. Ease the Guilt

If they had to make the decision to put their pet to sleep, or they're replaying what they could have done differently, guilt often sits right next to the grief. Speak to it gently.

"You gave [Name] a good life and a gentle goodbye. Making that call is one of the hardest, kindest things anyone can do for an animal they love."
Send as Card ✨

5. The Quiet House

The first evenings are often the hardest, because that's when the routine used to happen. Offer company for exactly that.

"I know the house probably feels really quiet right now. If you want company tonight, even just on the phone, I'm here."
Send as Card ✨

6. Offer Something Concrete

"Let me know if you need anything" hands them a job. A specific offer they can simply accept is much easier.

"I'm dropping dinner off tomorrow around 6. You don't need to answer the door or text back. I'll leave it on the step."
Send as Card ✨

7. The Later Check-In

Sympathy for a pet tends to fade fast, often within a day or two. A message a few weeks later shows you remember when almost no one else does.

"Thinking of you and [Name] today. I know it's been a few weeks, but I haven't forgotten. How are you doing, really?"

Send something they can keep.

A text gets buried in a chat. Send an anonymous, beautifully designed card they can come back to on the hard days.

Why These Messages Help

Grief over a pet is often brushed aside, so the person carrying it can feel alone in it. Each of these texts pushes back on that in a small way: naming the pet says they mattered, asking for a memory says you want to hear about them, and checking in later says the loss didn't stop counting after the first day. None of them try to fix anything. They just make it a little less lonely.

What *Not* to Text

  • "It was just a dog." (Or cat, or bird. To them it was never "just" anything.)
  • "When are you getting another one?" (A new pet isn't a replacement, and the question makes it sound like one.)
  • "At least it wasn't a person." (Comparing losses tells them their grief is too big.)
  • "I know exactly how you feel, when my dog died..." (Sharing can come later. Right now, keep the focus on their pet.)

You don't need perfect words. Saying their pet's name and letting them be sad is enough.
